Who may use the alpha

Local loopback development may use open registration to test separate accounts. Any external candidate alpha must be invitation-only and for adults aged 18 or older. Candidate matching remains a private, candidate-controlled workspace. Information and purposes

  • Account, personas and security: email, display name, password hash, verification/status, account-owned persona names, sessions, rate-limit and essential connection records—to admit and secure accounts and keep each account's persona workspaces separate.
  • Compass: your text, structured work preferences/experience, corrections, removals and source excerpts—to create an editable candidate profile for the active persona and candidate-only recommendations.
  • Optional CV: the original file is parsed on WorkLifeBalance's server and is not retained or sent to OpenAI. Locally redacted, user-selected experience/skills text may be sent to OpenAI only after a separate affirmative action.
  • Job search and matching: saved public jobs, references, candidate-only match evidence, commute estimates, feedback and application states—to provide the requested workspace.
  • Product feedback: the area, topic, optional 1–5 rating, product-only message, optional permission to follow up at the account email, triage status and an internal operator note—to review and improve the private alpha. Do not include job URLs, Compass or CV content, application text, provider diagnostics, secrets or another person's information. Product feedback is never sent to AI, model evaluation, analytics or advertising.
  • Optional semantic match evidence: for up to the configured bounded number of jobs, only confirmed criteria left unresolved by deterministic matching and canonical public job facts with short source quotes may be sent to OpenAI. Account/persona metadata, raw Compass answers, CV text, private ratings, home location and full advertisements are excluded. Validated assessments and evidence references are retained with the match for local rescoring.
  • Job-poster access: account ID, work email, stated employer, company website/domain, authority request, operator review status, reviewer, reason and timestamps—to assess whether an account may publish for the named organisation and to prevent impersonation, fraud and abuse. Approval is a limited access decision, not a guarantee that the organisation or every vacancy is genuine.
  • Public vacancies supplied by an approved poster: employer name, title, description, location/work model, application URL or email, optional source URL, status and timestamps—to store, review, publish, update or withdraw the requested vacancy. Published fields are deliberately public and may be retrieved by candidates like other public-job evidence. They are not joined to candidate accounts or disclosed to the poster as candidate activity.
  • Business contact data in a posted vacancy: a poster may include only application contact details that the poster is authorised to publish for that vacancy. A business email, role or named contact can still be personal data. The operator must establish the relevant controller roles, lawful basis and Articles 13 or 14 transparency route before hosted publication; local testing must use non-production data until that assessment is complete.
  • Private employer context: your private employer rating and short research note may be attached only after WorkLifeBalance has a sufficiently unique employer identity. They remain private to the active persona, are not attached to placeholder employers such as “Unknown”, are not published and are not sent to an LLM. They may affect candidate-side matching only as a user-controlled private signal.
  • Application contact and process guidance: a name, business role, published business email or phone number, postal address, application channel, requested documents, limits, deadline or instruction may be extracted only when it is expressly published in the user-requested job advertisement or the employer's official careers page. Extraction and display occur locally or on the operator-controlled application server; no configured LLM receives these contact fields. The source and uncertainty remain visible; WorkLifeBalance does not search private profiles, enrich the person from unrelated sources, contact them automatically or treat a missing detail as known.
  • Optional salary orientation: when configured, when the active persona has a compensation criterion, and when an advertisement states no pay, a bounded set of public job titles, countries/coarse job locations, summaries and limited role, experience, employment or weekly-hours facts may be sent to OpenAI Responses web search to look for attributable country-aware benchmarks. The adapter does not receive your salary target, Compass/profile facts, persona/account details, home location or other candidate data. Returned country, original currency, pay period, EUR-normalised range and source links are stored with the match evidence.
  • Optional application drafting: the selected public job's relevant facts and evidence, text-relevant application rules and minimised confirmed Compass facts are sent to OpenAI only when you ask for a cover letter. Contact names, identifying roles, email addresses, phone numbers and delivery URLs stay inside WorkLifeBalance and are removed from every drafting-prompt surface. The model receives no contact gender and is instructed not to infer it. The returned editable draft and your later edits are stored in the active persona's workspace for up to 180 days after the last update, unless you delete them earlier. WorkLifeBalance does not submit the application.
  • Operations: provider, purpose, timing, units, status, cost estimate and security events—to operate, protect and diagnose the service. WorkLifeBalance's provider ledger does not store prompts, CV/profile text, search terms, locations or provider responses.

AI processing and profiling

Your Compass text is sent to the OpenAI API to propose structured statements. Optional selected CV text may also be sent after the on-screen preview and separate action. When configured job extraction uses OpenAI, WorkLifeBalance first deterministically extracts application guidance inside its own application boundary and removes detected contact names, identifying roles, email addresses, phone numbers, postal addresses and URLs from the public advertisement text supplied to the provider. Free-text minimisation can still miss unexpected indirect identifiers, which is one reason external-alpha use remains gated. For optional semantic match evidence, OpenAI receives only each job's still-unresolved confirmed criteria and canonical public facts with short quotes; deterministic results are not sent for re-judgment, and the provider cannot assign scores or dispositions. When you deliberately create, recreate, lengthen or shorten a cover letter, OpenAI receives the selected job's relevant facts/evidence, text-relevant application rules, the existing draft where needed, and only minimised confirmed Compass facts needed for that task. The same contact-data minimisation is applied to every drafting-prompt surface. A safe salutation is constructed inside WorkLifeBalance from an exact source-published form of address or your explicit choice; WorkLifeBalance does not infer gender from a person's name and does not ask the model to do so. For configured salary research, OpenAI receives only the bounded public-job fields described above and must not receive the candidate's compensation criterion or any profile data. OpenAI acts as a processor under the operator's API agreement. API data is not used to train OpenAI models by default unless the operator opts in; the alpha must not opt in. Default API abuse-monitoring logs may contain prompts and outputs for up to 30 days unless an approved shorter-retention control applies.

The matching engine compares confirmed statements with public job evidence for you. Matches are recommendations exclusively for the candidate. First-party vacancies enter the same candidate-side corpus; the poster cannot select an audience or see who receives or opens a recommendation. A salary orientation is separately labelled as estimated, cites its sources and remains unknown matching evidence rather than a hard pass or fail. WorkLifeBalance does not apply German data to jobs in another country and abstains if the country, occupation mapping or evidence is not adequately supported. The result is not an employer decision and not a decision with legal or similarly significant effect. You can inspect, correct, remove, export and delete your information.

EU AI Act boundary

The intended matching product remains a candidate-side assistant: it recommends jobs only to the candidate, does not rank or filter candidates for an employer, gives posters, employers and recruiters no access to candidate profiles or scores, and does not submit applications. The separate publication prototype lets an approved poster submit one public vacancy but gives the poster no targeting controls or candidate analytics. Annex III point 4(a) of the EU AI Act expressly includes AI used for recruitment or selection, including placing targeted job advertisements. Whether candidate-side ranking that includes first-party vacancies remains outside that category depends on intended purpose and actual operation; no legal classification is asserted here. A documented intended-purpose and AI Act classification memorandum, qualified review and updated transparency assessment remain mandatory before hosted publication or an external alpha opens.

Prohibited sensitive data

Do not enter or upload health/disability, racial or ethnic origin, political views, religion or beliefs, trade-union membership, genetic/biometric identification, sex-life or sexual-orientation information, criminal convictions/offences, or security-clearance/background-check information. Apparent sensitive CV content is rejected locally rather than intentionally profiled or sent to OpenAI. Screening can make mistakes, so you must review the displayed text.

Legal bases

Accounts, account-owned personas, Compass, private employer context, candidate matching, saved workspaces, configured public-job salary research, user-requested application drafting and user-requested coarse routing provisionally rely on Article 6(1)(b) GDPR to provide the requested service, subject to counsel confirming necessity and the exact provider/source configuration. Job-poster account administration and a poster's requested publication may also provisionally rely on Article 6(1)(b), while authority checks, fraud prevention, moderation and narrowly limited security records may require a documented Article 6(1)(f) legitimate-interest assessment. Concrete legal record duties rely on Article 6(1)(c). These are draft mappings, not blanket legal bases: each purpose, data category and role must be recorded before hosting.

Processing a third person's source-published or poster-supplied business application contact cannot simply be placed on the candidate's or poster's contract basis. Hosted use requires a documented Article 6 assessment covering purpose, necessity, reasonable expectations and safeguards, plus the applicable Article 13 notice where data come from the person or Article 14 notice where they come from another source. An Article 14 concept must identify the public or poster source, data categories, recipients, retention, rights and a practical objection/removal channel; no exception for disproportionate effort may be assumed without a documented assessment. The optional CV-to-OpenAI transfer uses a separate, specific Article 6(1)(a) action and can be declined without losing ordinary Compass access.

Purely personal local use by a natural person may fall within the GDPR household exemption. Because a job-search tool can have a professional connection, WorkLifeBalance does not rely on that exemption as its server-ready compliance basis, and it must not be assumed for an operated private alpha or public service.

Reading or acknowledging this notice is not consent to unrelated processing. There are no analytics or marketing cookies in the alpha.

Public-vacancy platform boundary

Storing a vacancy supplied by a poster and making it available to an unlimited public audience may qualify as a hosting service or online platform under the Digital Services Act (DSA). That classification and the operator's establishment must be reviewed before hosting. The launch design must include clear content restrictions and enforcement information in the terms (Article 14), an accessible electronic notice-and-action channel for alleged illegal content (Article 16), and reasoned moderation decisions where required (Article 17).

Article 19 may exempt a qualifying micro or small online-platform provider from some additional Section 3 duties, including the Article 20 internal complaint system, unless it is a very large online platform. That possible exemption does not remove the earlier hosting-service duties in Articles 14, 16 and 17 and must not be assumed without documenting enterprise status and service classification. Until qualified review, notice/action, moderation records, reasons and any required appeal route are operational, vacancy publication remains local-only and hosted publication must fail closed.

Recipients and transfers

  • Netcup: hosts the selected EU server and database as processor after an Article 28 agreement is executed.
  • Cloudflare: provides DNS, proxy/TLS and abuse protection and processes technical connection/HTTP data under its customer DPA; depending on configuration, metadata and traffic processing can occur outside the EEA under the DPA's transfer safeguards.
  • OpenAI Ireland: processes selected Compass/CV text, contact-minimised public advertisement text used for configured extraction, unresolved confirmed criteria paired with canonical public facts for bounded semantic match evidence, minimised confirmed Compass and job facts plus text-relevant rules used for user-requested cover letters, bounded public job fields used for configured salary research, and the corresponding outputs through the API under its DPA. WorkLifeBalance removes detected contact names, identifying roles, email addresses, phone numbers, postal addresses and URLs before job extraction and applies the same minimisation to every cover-letter prompt surface. Private employer ratings and notes are excluded as well. It does not deliberately supply a contact identity or infer gender for a salutation. Transfers outside the EEA use adequacy decisions or Standard Contractual Clauses as applicable.
  • Salary benchmark sources: the current allowlist contains official EU and national employment/statistics sources and an attributable European Central Bank source for necessary currency conversion. No secondary salary source is currently enabled; a future secondary source requires prior methodological/provider review and corroboration. Those independent source sites may receive ordinary search/retrieval request data through OpenAI, but not your account, profile or salary target. External-alpha use requires an approved source policy and transfer/retention review and does not promise coverage of every European country or occupation.
  • Search and route providers: receive only task-specific generic search terms or the user-chosen coarse search/route anchor, mode and time. When configured, OpenWebNinja JSearch receives bounded role/location or remote-search phrases and returns public vacancy data from Google for Jobs, job boards and employer career pages. The optional Apify EURES actor receives bounded role keywords, country codes and a result limit and returns public EURES vacancies. The separate JobsPipe integration sends up to four short independent one-keyword searches: each uses either a title term or a description/skill term, most add an offline-resolved country code, and one may omit the country so that WorkLifeBalance can rank credible cross-border results locally; remote-only choice, freshness and split result limits may also be sent, but the raw city/postcode is not. The separate AgentX All Jobs integration starts one actor run with one role keyword, the user-chosen coarse location, an offline-resolved full country name, optional platform/remote/radius/freshness choices, a per-platform result target, a separate readback/presentation limit and a pay-per-event charge ceiling; WorkLifeBalance polls that same run, fetches its bounded dataset and never starts an automatic paid retry, while contact, email, phone and social fields returned by the actor are discarded. Apify, each community actor and their upstream source/service chains require separate review even when they share one local token. None of these adapters receives an account identifier, persona name, CV, complete Compass record or private rating; users should choose a city, postcode or nearby transport/place anchor rather than a home address. These integrations remain local evaluation paths until OpenWebNinja, Apify, each actor and applicable source/reuse terms have passed the contract, role, location, transfer, retention, deletion and security review.
  • Public job/ATS sites: receive ordinary technical web-request data when WorkLifeBalance retrieves a user-requested public job page. A public vacancy or public application contact is evidence for the candidate's own application preparation, not consent to automated or unrelated outreach.
  • Public visitors and candidates: receive the fields deliberately published in an approved first-party vacancy. They never receive the poster's private verification request, reviewer notes or account security data; posters never receive candidate workspace data.

Retention

  • invitations: 72 hours; recovery links: 60 minutes; sessions: no more than 30 days;
  • raw Compass answers and conversation text: 30 days after successful extraction;
  • pending/inferred facts: 30 days; rejected facts: 7 days;
  • CV binary: never stored; locally selected CV text: deleted when extraction finishes;
  • provider telemetry and ordinary application security events: 90 days; WorkLifeBalance does not retain Caddy or Uvicorn HTTP access logs in the reviewed private-alpha deployment. Caddy can emit operational runtime/error metadata, but its logger removes the complete HTTP request object (including URI and query string) before encoding; every container log uses size-based rotation of at most five 10 MB files, not a promised time-based period. Cloudflare and Netcup technical connection records follow their separately verified processor settings and periods;
  • saved editable application drafts: 180 days after their last update, or earlier when you delete them;
  • product feedback and its private triage record: 180 days after the last update, or earlier when you delete the feedback or account;
  • source-grounded application contacts and process instructions: retained only with the corresponding saved job and removed with that job or account; automatic source-staleness expiry and processor-side retention must be verified before external-alpha use;
  • job-poster verification requests, authority evidence and moderation records: local test data only for now; a hosted retention period tied to the last publication, withdrawal, dispute and statutory limitation needs must be documented, minimised, implemented and tested before launch;
  • poster-supplied vacancies: removed from public access when withdrawn, access is revoked or moderation requires removal; deletion/anonymisation of the underlying record and backups must follow a documented, tested schedule rather than indefinite retention;
  • private employer ratings and notes: retained only for the active persona and a sufficiently unique employer identity, then removed with that employer context, persona or account;
  • salary benchmark result, country/currency/period and source URLs: retained with the corresponding match, then deleted with the persona/account or under the general inactivity limit; OpenAI and source-site technical records follow the separately reviewed provider/source periods;
  • other confirmed profile/workspace data: while active, with notice after 12 months inactivity and deletion after 13 months unless you return or a concrete legal hold applies;
  • encrypted database backups: 14 rolling days.

The application implements the established candidate-workspace periods locally. Poster verification, moderation and vacancy-record expiry are not represented here as production-ready until their automated deletion and backup ageing have been tested. The alpha remains closed until job retention, disabled application access logging, backup expiry and processor-side connection-data retention have been verified in staging.

Your choices and rights

You can correct or remove Compass statements, source-grounded application guidance and private evidence values; edit or delete application drafts; review and delete your own product feedback; download a compact JSON copy of your account, personas and each current Compass; and delete the account in Account. The compact self-service download deliberately leaves out operational task, provider, match, feedback-triage and revision histories; it does not narrow a broader access or portability request. An approved poster can correct or withdraw its vacancy and request correction or deletion of verification data. A candidate, contact person or other affected person must also have a practical route to report a vacancy, object to or correct business contact data, and request removal. Depending on the circumstances, GDPR rights include access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, withdrawal of consent for future optional CV processing, and a complaint to a supervisory authority. Contact the privacy address above for a broader rights request; proportionate identity verification may be required.

Account deletion removes the private live-data aggregate. Before launch, rolling backups must be encrypted off-server and verified to age out within 14 days. Public job advertisements may remain in the shared public corpus where they contain no private profile or link to you.

Changes

Material changes receive a new notice version. The local job-poster prototype described above is not permission to host it. Hosted vacancy publication, candidate targeting or employer analytics, automated application, a sensitive-data purpose, a new provider, training/evaluation use or indefinite retention requires a fresh legal/DPIA and, where applicable, DSA and AI Act review before activation.
