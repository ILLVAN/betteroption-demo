# Domain and product-name decision — v0.7.2h

Status: documentation-only product judgement, dated 5 August 2026. No product,
UI, deployment, logo, email identity, package-version, or canonical-domain
change is authorized by this note.

## Decision

Keep `betteroption.example` as the canonical name through the private alpha.
That decision is based on comprehension and trust, not on the fact that the
domain is already owned. It tells a stressed job seeker what the product is for
before any explanation, while `.ai` makes the otherwise literal name feel
deliberate.

`suiting.work` is the strongest new challenger and the only newly proposed
domain worth a defensive purchase or a controlled naming test. It is short,
distinctive, and supports the useful idea “work that fits your life.” It is not
yet a safe rename: in ordinary English, the noun *suiting* means
[fabric for suits or a suit of clothes](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/suiting),
and “suiting work” is not an established phrase. A tagline can unlock the
metaphor, but the audience—not the project team—must prove that it does.

The decisive shortlist is:

1. `betteroption.example` — clearest and most reassuring current product name.
2. `suiting.work` — best concise, ownable, and economical challenger.
3. `lifelife.work` — boldest life-first personality, with a real spelling and
   collision tax.
4. `balancedwork.life` — most professional alternative, but generic and already
   adjacent to an established career/coaching business.
5. `movingon.work` — excellent transition language that narrows the product to
   leaving or outplacement.
6. `geta.life` — unforgettable campaign voice, but too easily read as an insult
   for the primary trust brand.

Do not rename the UI or canonical domain in `v0.7.2h`. If one exploratory domain
is bought, buy `suiting.work`, set a decision deadline, and test it against the
current name. Do not build a domain collection in place of learning.

## How the names were judged

The order balances five product questions rather than rewarding novelty alone:

- **Comprehension:** can a job seeker infer the promise before seeing a tagline?
- **Trust:** would the name feel credible during an anxious, consequential job
  search?
- **Distinctiveness:** can the product own a useful mental association rather
  than merely describe a category?
- **Transmission:** can someone hear it once, spell it, type the dot correctly,
  and remember it a day later?
- **Commercial durability:** does it stretch from Compass creation through job
  discovery, matching, applications, and later subscriptions without forcing
  the product to look like only a job board?

Price and collision risk can break a close tie, but neither rescues a weak
product name. The competitor review below is a current desk scan, not trademark,
company-name, or legal clearance.

## New-candidate conclusions

### `suiting.work` is the pearl, with one real caveat

This is the only new candidate that improves several dimensions at once:
length, distinction, visual rhythm, price, and breadth. Unlike a `.jobs` name,
it can still cover Compass building, matching, applications, and a later
work-life product. The decisive risk is linguistic, not competitive. The first
meaning many English speakers meet is tailoring. The name therefore wins a
test slot, not a rename.

### The `.jobs` names are expensive category labels

`worklife.jobs`, `easypeasy.jobs`, and `suiting.jobs` all explain “jobs,” but
that clarity comes with two costs. First, their supplied prices are roughly
€199–€203.88 per year. Second, the suffix frames the service as a vacancy board
when its defensible value is the personal Compass and evidence-bound matching.
The supplied email-forwarding feature only routes incoming mail. It is not a
mailbox, verified business identity, or transactional-email service.

`worklife.jobs` is the most professional of the three, yet the `Worklife`
landscape is already crowded. `easypeasy.jobs` is catchier, but its playful tone
and the exact-sector `easy.jobs` proximity make it a poor risk-adjusted bet.
`suiting.jobs` costs about twenty times the supplied annual price of
`suiting.work` while weakening the broader product story.

### `ebbelangts.jobs` is memorable for the wrong product

The phrase belongs to a Frankfurt anti-inflation and crisis movement whose own
site describes demonstrations, redistribution demands, and “enough is enough.”
That may be powerful political language, but it does not promise thoughtful job
fit. Outside the dialect region, users must also learn the missing apostrophe,
pronunciation, and spelling. It is the clearest reject in this round.

terms. A displayed “still free” availability result is not a reservation.

## The test that can authorize a rename

Use 15–20 people resembling the initial job-seeker audience. Test
`betteroption.example`, `suiting.work`, and `lifelife.work` without revealing the
project team's preference.

1. Show only the name and ask, “What do you think this product does?”
2. Say the domain once and ask the participant to type it. Record repeated-word,
   dot, and spelling errors.
3. Show identical landing-page copy and design. Measure trust and willingness to
   upload or describe career preferences—not only which name feels cool.
4. Ask for unaided recall and spelling after 24 hours.
5. Ask whether the name would look credible in an email, invoice, browser tab,
   recommendation to a friend, and job-search context.

A rename requires a clear win over the current name in both comprehension and
trust, plus acceptable spoken accuracy. Recall alone is insufficient. Before
shipping the winner, perform current EUIPO/DPMA and target-market trademark,
company-name, domain-history, social-handle, app-store, and email-deliverability
checks with professional advice where appropriate.

## Explicit non-actions

- No UI, logo, legal-name, email, social, package, or deployment rename.
- No redirect or canonical-domain switch.
- No assumption that a registrar availability screen equals clearance.
- No claim that an existing collision automatically proves infringement; the
  links are product-risk signals for a later professional review.
- No decision based solely on money already spent on `betteroption.example`.
